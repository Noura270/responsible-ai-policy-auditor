EU AI Act - Summary of Key Principles for High-Risk AI Systems

This is an original, condensed summary written for internal governance-assessment
use. It is NOT a reproduction of the official legal text of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689
and should not be treated as legal advice or a substitute for the official regulation.

1. Risk-Based Classification
The EU AI Act classifies AI systems into four tiers based on the risk they pose to
people: Unacceptable risk (prohibited outright - e.g. social scoring of individuals,
manipulative techniques), High-risk (subject to strict obligations - e.g. AI used in
recruitment, credit scoring, education, law enforcement), Limited risk (transparency
obligations only - e.g. chatbots must disclose they are AI), and Minimal risk (no
specific obligations).

2. Obligations for High-Risk AI Systems
Providers and deployers of high-risk AI systems must maintain a risk management
system throughout the system's lifecycle, ensure data governance practices for
training data (relevance, representativeness, error checking), maintain technical
documentation, enable automatic logging of events (traceability), and design the
system for appropriate human oversight.

3. Human Oversight
High-risk AI systems must be designed so that natural persons can effectively
oversee their operation, including the ability to understand system output, decide
not to use the system in a particular situation, and intervene in or stop the
system's operation.

4. Transparency Obligations
People must be informed when they are interacting with an AI system (unless obvious
from context), and when AI-generated content could be mistaken for genuine human
content, appropriate disclosure is required.

5. Accountability Across the AI Value Chain
Responsibility is distributed across providers (who build the system) and deployers
(who put it into use) - deployers of high-risk systems have their own obligations,
including using the system according to its instructions and monitoring its
operation, not just relying on the provider's assurances.

6. Third-Party and Vendor Considerations
When an organization deploys an AI system built or provided by an external vendor,
it does not automatically transfer away governance responsibility. Deployers are
expected to understand the risk classification of vendor-provided systems and
apply appropriate oversight measures.

Key Governance Concepts Embedded in This Regulation:
- Risk-proportionate obligations: the higher the potential harm, the stricter the
  requirements - a minimal-risk chatbot is not held to the same standard as a
  high-risk hiring tool.
- Human agency is a design requirement, not an afterthought: oversight must be
  built in, not bolted on.
- Transparency is a baseline right for people interacting with AI, not an optional
  courtesy.
