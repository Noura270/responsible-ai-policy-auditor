GDPR - Summary of Key Principles Relevant to AI Systems

This is an original, condensed summary written for internal governance-assessment
use, focused on the aspects of the General Data Protection Regulation most relevant
to AI systems. It is NOT a reproduction of the official legal text and should not
be treated as legal advice.

1. Lawful Basis for Processing
Personal data may only be processed if there is a valid lawful basis - typically
consent, contractual necessity, legal obligation, or legitimate interest. An AI
system that processes personal data (e.g. resumes, customer records) without an
identifiable lawful basis is operating outside GDPR requirements.

2. Data Minimization
Only the personal data that is actually necessary for the specified purpose should
be collected and processed. Sending an entire dataset (e.g. full resumes with all
fields) to a downstream AI process when only a subset of fields is needed violates
this principle.

3. Purpose Limitation
Personal data collected for one purpose (e.g. customer support) should not be
reused for an unrelated purpose (e.g. marketing profiling) without a new lawful
basis or the data subject's awareness.

4. Transparency and the Right to Information
Data subjects have the right to know that their personal data is being processed,
including when it is shared with third parties (such as an external AI vendor) and
for what purpose. Silent or undisclosed data sharing with third-party AI providers
is a common GDPR gap in AI systems.

5. Automated Decision-Making (Article 22)
Individuals have the right not to be subject to a decision based solely on
automated processing, including profiling, that produces legal or similarly
significant effects on them - unless specific conditions are met (e.g. explicit
consent, contractual necessity with safeguards). Meaningful human review of
automated decisions is a key safeguard.

6. Data Subject Rights
Individuals have rights to access their data, request correction, request erasure
("right to be forgotten"), and object to certain processing. AI systems that
retain personal data indefinitely without a documented retention policy create
exposure here.

7. Third-Party Data Sharing and Processor Obligations
When personal data is shared with an external AI provider (a data processor),
contractual safeguards (a Data Processing Agreement) and due diligence on that
provider's own data protection practices are expected.

Key Governance Concepts Embedded in This Regulation:
- Necessity over convenience: collect and share the minimum data required, not
  everything available.
- Consent and awareness are not optional extras: data subjects should know how
  their data is used, especially when AI and third parties are involved.
- Automated decisions with real consequences for a person require a meaningful
  human safeguard, not just a technical audit trail.
